# Team video script (Groundtruth, solo)

Target: 45–55 s. Landscape, phone at eye level, soft light on your face (window or lamp in front of you, never behind). Send the raw clips; titles, name card, captions, music, the space shot opener and the end card get added in editing.

Fill in the [brackets] before recording. Say it like you're talking to a friend, not reading. If a line trips you up, reword it. Record each beat as its own clip, 2–3 takes each.

## Full read (for practice, about 40 s spoken)

Hey, I'm Tarun, and I'm the whole team behind Groundtruth. It tells you which housing laws apply at any address in the US.

I'm a [year] at UC Berkeley studying [major]. I mostly build [one thing].

Here's why I picked this. A renter gets told their rent is going up ten percent next month. Is that legal? The answer depends on the state, the city, how old the building is, and laws that keep changing. Most people just guess.

So in 24 hours I built a system that reads the actual laws, checks every rule against the law word for word, and works out what applies to each building, on any date.

I've had [number] energy drinks and zero teammates to blame.

Renters shouldn't need a lawyer to know their rights. That's what Groundtruth is for, and I'm not stopping here.

## Filming checklist

- Landscape, 1080p or 4K, 30 fps. Clean the lens.
- Light in front of you. Turn off overhead lights if they cast shadows under your eyes.
- Quiet room. Phone mic within about 1 m, or AirPods mic.
- Leave 1 s of silence before and after each line so cuts are clean.
- Background: something real (desk, laptop, whiteboard), not a blank wall.

## Reference: winning example (Biosecurity, Hack-Nation 6), transcribed

Team video, about 60 s. Structure: one-line hook naming the product and what it does → each person gives name, role, school, specialty (about 5 s each) → whiteboard "good vibes" beat ("Hi, I haven't slept in a while") → shared mission → "we're not stopping until this problem is solved" → logo and tagline.

> Hi, this is the team behind ambr, the AI platform that predicts antibiotic resistance directly from bacterial genomes. / What's up? My name is Daniel. I'm a postdoctoral researcher at Harvard Medical School specializing in data science. My focus is on statistical analysis. / I'm Tom, a postdoctoral researcher at MIT with a PhD in biophysics. I bring expertise in computational biology, mathematical modeling and AI to help translate complex biological data into predictive models for ambr. / Hi, we're Max and Rafael, visiting students at MIT researching trustworthy health machine learning, and we're doing the AI backbone. / Hi, I haven't slept in a while. / With a combination of our know-how, we're building ambr to save time but most importantly lives. We're starting our venture today and we're not stopping until this problem is solved.

Demo video, about 60 s. Structure: presenter on camera states the problem → 3D visuals of the problem → product one-liner → "let's jump straight into the demo" → narrated screen recording, one feature per caption → validation method → logo and tagline.

> Millions of people die each year because antibiotics no longer work. Resistance spreads fast. Antibiotics kill most bacteria. But resistant survivors return. Soon the drugs stop working and guessing is no longer enough. ambr predicts which antibiotics will fail before the lab does. Now let's jump straight into the demo. ambr reads resistance evidence straight from the genome. Upload an E. coli FASTA. In seconds it finds genes and mutations, then scores five antibiotics. Each drug gets a call and a confidence score. The reference structure highlights Serine 83. For ciprofloxacin, mechanism, determinants and target checks explain the call. Weak evidence stays a no call. Performance is measured with grouped five-fold validation designed to prevent data leakage. ambr. Save time. Save lives.

Groundtruth: which rental housing laws apply at this address, on any date?

US rental law is layered. State statutes, county rules and city ordinances each have their own coverage tests and effective dates, and the mailing city on an address isn't always the legal city. Renters, advocates and small landlords have to read municipal codes by hand to find out which rules apply to one building.

Groundtruth answers that at the level of a single address. Claude Opus 5.5 reads 70 state and city law documents and extracts 200 candidate rules. Every quote is checked word for word against its source (200 of 200 matched), and a consolidation pass merges the candidates into 55 rules. The Census geocoder places each of 500 sample buildings in its legal city, so a "Dorchester" address resolves to Boston. A deterministic engine, with no AI involved, then tests every rule against the building's facts: units, year built versus certificate-of-occupancy cutoffs, owner type, and state-versus-local precedence. Each rule comes back as applies, unknown, superseded, not yet effective or pending. Missing facts produce "unknown" with the missing fact named.

In the app you type an address and fly from orbit down to the building in Google's photorealistic 3D. Every rule shows its verbatim source text, and you can drag a date slider to watch laws take effect, such as New Jersey's FAIR Act in July 2027, which is flagged against Hoboken's local ban. You can ask a question in plain English and get an answer drawn only from the cited rules. You can also paste a new ordinance and see which of the 500 buildings it affects within about 30 seconds.

All five required change tests behave as specified. An AI-assisted audit of 27 sample addresses measured precision and recall of 0.98; the same sample guided fixes, so this is not held-out accuracy or an official score. Not legal advice.

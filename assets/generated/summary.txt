Groundtruth: which rental housing laws apply at this address, on any date?

US rental law is layered. State statutes, county rules and city ordinances each have their own coverage tests and effective dates, and the mailing city on an address isn't always the legal city. Renters and small landlords read municipal codes by hand to learn which rules apply to one building.

Claude Opus 5.5 reads 73 law documents (54 supplied, 19 captured link-only pages) and extracts 206 candidate rules. Every quote is checked word for word against its source (206 of 206 matched), and a consolidation pass merges the candidates into 59 rules. The Census geocoder places each of 500 sample buildings in its legal city, so a "Dorchester" address resolves to Boston. A deterministic engine, with no AI involved, then tests every rule against the building's facts: units, year built versus certificate-of-occupancy cutoffs, owner type, and state-versus-local precedence. Each rule comes back as applies, unknown, superseded, not yet effective or pending. Missing facts produce "unknown" with the missing fact named.

In the app you type an address and fly from orbit down to the building in Google's photorealistic 3D. Every rule shows its verbatim source text. Drag the date slider to watch laws take effect, such as New Jersey's FAIR Act in July 2027, which is flagged against Hoboken's local ban. You can ask a question in plain English and get an answer that cites the rules it uses. Paste a new ordinance and see which of the 500 buildings it affects in about 30 seconds.

All five required change tests behave as specified. An AI-assisted audit of 27 sample addresses found precision and recall of 0.98. That sample also guided fixes, so this is not held-out accuracy or an official score. Not legal advice.
